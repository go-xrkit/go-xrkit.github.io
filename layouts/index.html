Why go-xrkit

XR glasses expose their 3D mode as an ordinary display mode — a VITURE Beast reports 3840x1080 for side-by-side 3D and 1920x1200 for plain 2D. There is no XR SDK anywhere in the stereo path: turning that panel into a believable picture is arithmetic on orientation, eye layout and projection, done exactly and tested against known directions rather than discovered by putting the glasses on. Sign and axis-order mistakes are invisible in a still frame and awful to wear — pose once shipped with yaw composed before pitch, and every single-axis test passed while it did, because with one non-zero angle the order cannot matter.

Packages

A single Go module, github.com/go-xrkit/xrkit, split into focused packages — import only what you need.

pose

Quaternions, the Euler convention head trackers actually report, recentring ("this is straight ahead now") and smoothing for a noisy tracker at rest.

stereo

How a frame packs two eyes — mono, side-by-side, top-bottom — and an explicit Swapped flag, because eye-reversed material is not detectable from the pixels.

projection

Direction ↔ picture: a flat virtual screen, equirectangular (360×180 or VR180's 180×180), and equidistant fisheye (180° to 200°).

warp

The projection precomputed into a lookup table, so reshaping a frame for one eye costs a copy, not a per-pixel computation. Both eyes at 4K: ~3 ms; at 8K: ~8 ms — against a 16.6 ms budget at 60 Hz, with zero allocations per frame.

ribbon

Screens on a 360° band: where each one sits, and how they composite into one panorama by yaw. A yaw on an equirectangular panorama is exactly a horizontal shift, which is why turning the band costs nothing and the warp table is never rebuilt.

glasses

Which display is the headset, and how wide is the view. Every catalogue entry records how far it has been checked — Observed, Enumerated or Published — and you can declare your own model in an HCL file, where the figure you measured sits next to a comment saying what you measured it from.

The player

go-xrkit/player puts the geometry to use: xrplay film.mp4 detects the projection and layout from the file, decodes on the GPU, warps each eye, and shows the result full screen on the glasses' own display — joining go-macos/avfoundation for hardware decode and go-widgets/window for the window, neither of which knows anything about the other.

There is no head tracking yet: the VITURE Beast's IMU is not reachable over HID — its interfaces open and accept commands but emit nothing, proven against a control run that captured 481 reports from the same reader on other devices. That absence is exactly why warp is a precomputed table: with a fixed orientation, the sampling is identical on every frame.

Repositories

Four repositories with code: the geometry, and the three things built on it.

xrkit

The geometry above — pose, stereo, projection, warp, ribbon, glasses. Every package is testable with no headset attached, and is gated at 100% coverage.

player

xrplay film.mp4: 360°, VR180 and 3D films, per eye, full screen on the glasses' own display. Hardware decode, per-eye reprojection, no XR SDK anywhere in the stereo path.

desk

Several computer screens on the same 360° ribbon, scrolled from the keyboard. Each virtual display is created at exactly one eye's resolution and given exactly the arc that eye can see, so looking straight at a screen shows it edge to edge at one source pixel per output pixel. The numbers are not configured — they follow from the headset's optics.

android

Screen capture on Android, as an ordinary CGO_ENABLED=0 Go binary talking to a small Java host over a socket. It also documents, with transcripts, the four different ways Android 15 refuses to let an app create a display it can launch anything onto — and what it gives you instead.

Quality bar

  • Pure Go. CGO_ENABLED=0 across the org. No XR SDK, no native shims.
  • One dependency, and it is argued for. xrkit's go.mod requires HCL, and nothing else — because a figure about a headset is only worth something with its provenance attached, and HCL has comments: what you measured sits next to the number you measured it from. The geometry packages themselves pull in nothing.
  • 100% test coverage. Gated in CI, including error branches, every package testable without a headset attached.
  • Six architectures. Build + test on all six of Go's 64-bit targets — amd64 / arm64 natively, loong64 / riscv64 / ppc64le / s390x under qemu — because this is float64 geometry leaning on Atan2/Asin/Acos at their domain edges, and s390x is big-endian with a different FPU.
  • BSD-3-Clause on every source file.
