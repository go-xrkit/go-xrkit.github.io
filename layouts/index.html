XR glasses show one screen. This puts a ring of them around you — real displays the desktop extends onto, so ordinary applications run there, captured and drawn as flat screens on a band at eye level. The keyboard turns the band; one key changes what a panel shows.

Or one screen wider than the glasses. A spreadsheet does not fit a screen the size of one eye's view, so -wide 6400 makes every screen three and a third views across and you reach the rest by turning your head. The band stays FLAT while you do: measured, it draws one source column per destination column at every angle a head reaches, where a band of turned facets repeats and drops columns as soon as it moves. What curves is the reach — the radius of a cylinder that is never drawn.

The angles are not configured, they follow from the optics. A VITURE Beast in its side-by-side mode gets screens of 1920x1080 spanning 51.57° each; an XREAL 1S gets 46.06°. How MANY screens is a choice — six by default, nine at most. Manufacturers publish a diagonal field of view and a projection needs the horizontal, so the diagonal is stored exactly as published and the rest derived — and a headset whose figure cannot be sourced carries no figure at all. A wrong field of view has no symptom: everything renders, in the wrong place.

The distortion table is never rebuilt. Building one costs 56.5 ms and a frame has 16.6 — but on an equirectangular panorama a yaw is exactly a horizontal shift, so turning the ribbon happens where the screens are composited, where it costs nothing.

How any of this is known

Nothing here is claimed from reading a header. The field of view a manufacturer prints without saying which axis it is on was pinned down from their own arithmetic — a 171-inch screen at 4 metres subtends 57.00° on its diagonal, and XREAL publishes both numbers. The compositor is checked against an independent per-pixel reference that reuses nothing of the fast path. A capture is checked against an instrument that is not this code at all: a Windows desktop, read through DXGI and through GDI, agrees with QEMU's own framebuffer dump in 0 of 480 000 pixels. And where a platform refuses something, the refusal is quoted rather than paraphrased — Android was asked four different ways whether an ordinary application may put other apps on a display it creates, and the answer is in the repository, in Android's words.

What is not proven is written down too. Every repository that makes a hardware claim says which hardware was connected and exercised, which was only partially observed, and which is known solely from documentation — and never rounds one up to the next. If you want a headset supported and verified rather than quoted, send us one.
